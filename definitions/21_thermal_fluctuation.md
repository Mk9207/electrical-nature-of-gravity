## Definition 21: Thermal Fluctuation

### ■ Original Definition (Summary)

**Thermal fluctuation** refers to the temporary, irregular motion of particles (molecules, atoms, electrons, etc.) within a substance caused by thermal energy. It is a natural consequence of statistical mechanics and has the following characteristics:

- The higher the temperature, the greater the average kinetic energy of the particles, and thus, the larger the fluctuations.
- Thermal fluctuations cause variations in physical quantities such as energy, position, velocity, and pressure, impacting macroscopic phenomena.
- On a microscopic scale, it manifests as thermal noise or Brownian motion.
- Even in thermal equilibrium, local energy deviations persist.

This phenomenon is involved in a wide range of physical, chemical, and biological phenomena, including thermal noise (Johnson noise) in electronic devices and molecular diffusion within living organisms.
