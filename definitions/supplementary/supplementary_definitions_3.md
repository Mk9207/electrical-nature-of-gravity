# Supplementary Definitions (Group 3 / B) — Definitions 38–45

---

### 🔹 Definition 38: Electric Field Gradient
**Simplified Definition**: The rate of change in electric field strength within space; indicates variations in direction and magnitude.  
**Relation to Theory**: Used to interpret local gravitational fluctuations as “spatial electric potential slopes.” Gentle or sharp gradients in electric fields correspond to the acceleration behavior of objects.

---

### 🔹 Definition 39: Charging
**Simplified Definition**: The phenomenon in which an object gains electric charge through friction or electrostatic induction.  
**Relation to Theory**: Charging forms localized spatial potentials, influencing surrounding electric field structures and potentially triggering gravitational responses.

---

### 🔹 Definition 40: Electric Potential
**Simplified Definition**: A scalar value representing the potential energy per unit charge; defined by the integral of the electric field from a reference point.  
**Relation to Theory**: Serves as an alternative representation of gravitational potential, with electric field configurations reinterpreting inter-mass interactions (attraction).

---

### 🔹 Definition 41: Reflection
**Simplified Definition**: The bouncing back of waves or particles at a boundary; applicable to electromagnetic waves, sound, light, etc.  
**Relation to Theory**: When spatial structure changes reflect electric fields or charge distributions, interference patterns may emerge that affect local gravitational responses.

---

### 🔹 Definition 42: Resonance
**Simplified Definition**: A phenomenon where a system exhibits maximum response at certain frequencies; found across electrical, mechanical, and acoustic systems.  
**Relation to Theory**: When spatial electric field structures resonate with the electrical properties of matter, transient gravitational effects can become amplified.

---

### 🔹 Definition 43: Piezoelectric Effect
**Simplified Definition**: The phenomenon where mechanical pressure generates voltage, or vice versa.  
**Relation to Theory**: Pressure applied to matter modulates electric field structures, suggesting the potential for linked gravitational changes in localized space.

---

### 🔹 Definition 44: Tensor
**Simplified Definition**: A multidimensional quantity extending beyond scalars and vectors; composed of combinations of directions and components.  
**Relation to Theory**: A mathematical framework essential for structurally expressing and reconfiguring spatial electric fields, forming the foundation of the theory’s formal modeling.

---

### 🔹 Definition 45: Neutrality
**Simplified Definition**: A state where positive and negative charges are balanced, appearing to have no electric field.  
**Relation to Theory**: Neutral substances can still polarize or be modulated by external electric fields, thereby participating in spatial electric field = gravitational field interactions. “Neutrality” itself becomes a target for external manipulation.
