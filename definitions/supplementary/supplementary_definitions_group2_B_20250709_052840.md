# Supplementary Definitions Group 2 (Set B)

---

### 🔹 Definition 31: Resonance
**Simple Definition**: A phenomenon in which a system exhibits maximum response at a certain input frequency.  
**Relevance**: Resonant structures in spatial tensors and electric fields relate to gravitational waves and anti-gravity resonance, acting as triggers for dynamic structures.

---

### 🔹 Definition 32: Dielectric Breakdown
**Simple Definition**: A phenomenon in which an insulator transitions to a conductive state under high voltage.  
**Relevance**: Indicates the limit of electric field structures, serving as a model for “gravitational fault” changes or anomalous spatial responses.

---

### 🔹 Definition 33: Charge Density
**Simple Definition**: The density of electric charge distributed in space; the amount of charge per unit volume.  
**Relevance**: May serve as a source of local electric field strength and gravity, contributing to a unified model with mass density.

---

### 🔹 Definition 34: Moment
**Simple Definition**: A measure of the deviation from the center or contribution to rotation of a physical quantity.  
**Relevance**: Used as electric dipole moment or gravitational moment to analyze asymmetry in spatial structures quantitatively.

---

### 🔹 Definition 35: Thermal Noise
**Simple Definition**: Random signals or voltage fluctuations derived from thermal energy.  
**Relevance**: Investigated as spatial electric field fluctuations = gravitational jitter model; involved in equilibrium theories of spatial deformation.

---

### 🔹 Definition 36: Electric Double Layer
**Simple Definition**: A structure of positive and negative charge layers formed at the interface between electrode and electrolyte.  
**Relevance**: Considered a micro-model of local gravity structure formation via local electric field generation at spatial interfaces.

---

### 🔹 Definition 37: Field Effect
**Simple Definition**: A phenomenon where an external electric field affects the behavior of charge carriers within a material.  
**Relevance**: Fundamental model for changing material “gravitational response” via spatial electric field modulation; close to the basis of mass modulation through electric fields.
