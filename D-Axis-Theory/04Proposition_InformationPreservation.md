# 🧱 Proposition 04: Reconstruction of the Law of Information Preservation and D-Axis Reversibility

> **Proposition 04: What is perceived as "information loss" in Structure A is merely unobservable, and the information is "reversibly retained" in Structure D.**

---

## Chapter 1: Proposition Summary

This proposition redefines the claim that information absorbed by a black hole (Structure A) is "lost," by asserting that within the D-axis structure, information is completely preserved and structurally encapsulated on the D-side.

This preservation is reversible and merely exists in a **state of intra-structural transition beyond the observational system** due to superluminal spatial transfer—not because the information is destroyed.

---

## Chapter 2: Conflict and Divergence with Conventional Theories

Conventional theories contain the following contradictions:

| Theoretical Framework | Claim | Issue |
|------------------------|-------|-------|
| General Relativity | Information "disappears" inside black holes | Contradiction with the law of information preservation |
| Quantum Theory | Information must always be preserved | Cannot visually verify information inside black holes |

D-axis theory reconstructs this conflict as follows:

- Structure A functions as an **information absorption and compression device**
- Information is **transferred toward Structure D**
- The transfer exceeds the speed of light and becomes **unobservable**
- → Information is merely "unseen," not "lost"

---

## Chapter 3: Definition of Reversibility and Preservation in the D-Axis Structure

Reversibility in the context of the D-axis structure is defined as follows:

| Item | Explanation |
|------|-------------|
| **Preservation** | Information maintains a “structurally encapsulated state” through A→D transfer |
| **Reversibility** | Information remains in a state that allows "re-output" or "re-observation" |
| **Unobservability** | Due to superluminal transfer, visibility is temporarily cut off from within space-time |

Thus, the focus is not on “where” the information is, but “within which structure” it is preserved.

---

## Chapter 4: Misconception of "Loss" Due to Observational Limitations

Information absorbed into Structure A is misperceived as "lost" for the following reasons:

1. **After being absorbed, information is transferred to Structure D**
2. **This transfer is faster than the speed of light, rendering it unobservable from outside**
3. **Until it reappears in Structure D, the information is in an “unobservable state”**

This "unobservable state" simply refers to an **ongoing intra-structural transfer within the D-axis structure**, not a disappearance into external void.

Moreover, **the information that reaches Structure D is retained in a state reproducible by its structural properties**.  
This is not a reconstruction from nothing, but **a reappearance of already-retained information**, merely requiring the re-connection of visibility and causality.

---

## Chapter 5: Conclusion and Unified Structure

In the D-axis structure, information is not "destroyed" but "compressed and transferred."  
Unobservability is a **misperception caused by observational limitations**, not the result of destruction.

Thus, the black hole information loss problem is redefined as follows:

> ✅ Information compressed and transferred by Structure A is retained in Structure D  
> ✅ Information becomes "unobservable" due to superluminal transfer, but is structurally preserved  
> ✅ Reversibility means "being in a reproducible state within Structure D," and involves no destruction or loss
