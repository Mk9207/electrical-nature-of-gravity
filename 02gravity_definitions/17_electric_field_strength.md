
# 🔹 Definition 17: Electric Field Strength

## ■ Original Definition (Summary)

**Electric field strength** is a vector quantity that represents the magnitude of the force experienced by a unit positive charge at a given point in space. It is defined by the equation:

- **Definition**:  
  **E = F / q**  
  where E is the electric field strength (V/m), F is the force acting on the charge (N), and q is the charge (C).

- **Relation to Potential**:  
  **E = −∇V**  
  The electric field is the spatial gradient of electric potential. The steeper the potential change, the stronger the electric field in that direction.

- **Sources**:  
  Electric fields are generated by point charges, line charges, surface charges, and distributed charge structures.

## ■ Relevance to the Theory (Gravity as an Electric Phenomenon)

In this theory, which views gravity as an electric phenomenon, **electric field strength (E)** is redefined as equivalent to gravitational acceleration and the spatial structure of gravitational attraction.

### 1. **Reconstructing Gravitational Acceleration**
A model is proposed where traditional gravitational acceleration g is equated with E:
- g ≈ 9.8 m/s² on Earth's surface is considered to arise from the spatial electric potential gradient (−∇V) generated by Earth's internal structure.
- Thus, **g ≡ E** is a possible new definition of gravity.

### 2. **Superposition of Mass and Electric Fields in Space**
Massive objects locally alter the spatial electric field structure through the charges or polarity within their structure.
- Even minor differences in electron density can induce broad gravitational (electric field) variations.
- The perceived "strength" of gravity depends heavily on the background electric field.

### 3. **Vector Nature and Directionality of Gravity**
Just like electric fields, gravity has directionality and vector properties. Its direction and magnitude vary according to the spatial electric potential.
- Phenomena such as inclined gravitational pull near certain structures can be explained through electric field vector behavior.

### 4. **Theoretical Basis for Antigravity Field Formation**
Artificial alteration of electric potential structures may allow for the inversion of E (≒ g).
- This enables construction of spatial fields where objects "float" instead of falling.
