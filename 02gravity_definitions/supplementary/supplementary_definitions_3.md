# 🔹 Supplementary Definitions: Group 3 (Set B) — Definitions 38–45

---

## 🔹 Definition 38: Electric Field Gradient

**Simplified Definition:**  
The rate of change in electric field strength across space, indicating how rapidly its magnitude and direction vary.

**Relevance to This Theory:**  
Gravitational variations are modeled as **spatial electric potential slopes**. A strong or weak electric field gradient corresponds directly to the **acceleration behavior of nearby objects**, forming the basis for directional gravitational modeling.

---

## 🔹 Definition 39: Charging

**Simplified Definition:**  
The process by which an object gains electric charge through friction, contact, or electrostatic induction.

**Relevance to This Theory:**  
Charging produces **localized potential zones**, which reshape surrounding electric field structures. These structures may initiate **gravitational responses**, enabling potential control over mass interactions.

---

## 🔹 Definition 40: Electric Potential

**Simplified Definition:**  
A scalar quantity representing the potential energy per unit charge at a given point; derived as the line integral of the electric field from a reference location.

**Relevance to This Theory:**  
Electric potential is interpreted as **gravitational potential** in this model. The **interactions between masses** are redefined as field effects stemming from potential gradients in the electric domain.

---

## 🔹 Definition 41: Reflection

**Simplified Definition:**  
The rebounding of waves, particles, or fields at a boundary between two media.

**Relevance to This Theory:**  
Reflected electric fields or charge distributions at spatial boundaries can generate **interference patterns**, which alter **local gravitational field behavior**. This is vital for spatial manipulation and gravity shielding technologies.

---

## 🔹 Definition 42: Resonance

**Simplified Definition:**  
A condition in which a system responds most strongly at particular frequencies.

**Relevance to This Theory:**  
When spatial electric fields **resonate with material properties**, this enhances or destabilizes local gravitational behavior. Resonance becomes a key tuning method for **field amplification or neutralization**.

---

## 🔹 Definition 43: Piezoelectric Effect

**Simplified Definition:**  
The generation of electric voltage from mechanical pressure—or vice versa.

**Relevance to This Theory:**  
Pressure-induced voltage causes **modifications in internal electric field configurations**, potentially altering the gravitational properties of that region. This suggests a direct link between **mechanical stress and gravity modulation**.

---

## 🔹 Definition 44: Tensor

**Simplified Definition:**  
A mathematical object that generalizes scalars and vectors, capable of encoding multi-directional physical relationships.

**Relevance to This Theory:**  
Tensors are the **core formal language** for representing **electric field structures in space**, allowing precise modeling and transformation of **gravity-like behaviors** through mathematical manipulation.

---

## 🔹 Definition 45: Neutrality

**Simplified Definition:**  
A state in which positive and negative charges cancel out, leading to no net electric field.

**Relevance to This Theory:**  
Even neutral systems can be **externally polarized**, contributing to gravitational field variations. **Neutrality becomes manipulable**, making it an important control point for spatial field engineering and gravity regulation.
